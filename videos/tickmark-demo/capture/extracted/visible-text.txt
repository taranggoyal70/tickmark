[div] Why it gets cheaper
[h2] Learning that compiles, not learning that accumulates.
[p] Most agents “learn” by growing a prompt. Every lesson makes the next call longer, slower and more expensive, and none of it can be audited.
[p] Tickmark turns a correction into a
[span] rule
[p] : a predicate and an action, versioned, readable, and executable with no model call at all. The month the agent learns that AWS codes to 6820 split 60/25/15, that invoice stops costing anything to decide - forever.
[p] So the cost curve bends the right way. More learning means fewer calls, not bigger ones. And a controller can read the rule, disable it, or ask which corrections taught it - which is the difference between automation an auditor signs off and automation they refuse.
[div] The loop
[h2] Five steps, and a human owns one of them.
[div] Rulebook
[p] the weights - deterministic rules the agent has already earned
[div] Close run
[p] the forward pass - rules first, model only on the residue
[div] Corrections
[p] the loss signal - what the controller changed, captured structurally
[div] Gradient step
[p] distil corrections into candidate rules, backtested against every closed period
[div] The human gate
[p] a controller accepts or rejects. Analysis never writes.
[p] Two constraints are borrowed, with credit, from
[a] backpass
[p] : a rule needs evidence from at least two distinct corrections, and analysis never writes. In accounting those are not niceties - they are traceability and segregation of duties.
[div] What it actually does
[h2] Three steps of the close, end to end.
[h3] Bank reconciliation
[p] Ties the statement to the ledger across all four cardinalities - one payment, an ACH batch against six invoices, an invoice settled in two tranches, a lockbox deposit. Names every residual as fx, fee, partial or timing, or refuses the match.
[h3] AP invoice coding
[p] GL account, department, and class per invoice - including the split allocations that are pure tribal knowledge, like cloud spend carved 60/25/15 across Engineering, Data and Product.
[h3] Accrual completeness
[p] Finds the recurring cost that was incurred but never invoiced before cut-off, sizes it off trailing actuals, and books it as a balanced entry with a preparer and an approver who are not the same person.
[div] Guardrails
[h2] The parts that make it usable on real books.
[h3] Materiality is a hard gate
[p] Nothing above the threshold auto-posts, whatever the confidence. A $12 bank fee and a $2.4M wire do not get the same scrutiny.
[h3] Debits equal credits
[p] Checked arithmetically in the database, never taken from model output.
[h3] Preparer ≠ approver
[p] Enforced by a constraint, not a convention.
[h3] Tickmarks are append-only
[p] You supersede one with a new tickmark. You never edit or delete the record of a verification.
[h3] No rule without a backtest
[p] A proposed rule is replayed against every closed period, and the controller sees what it would have broken before adopting it.
[h3] Every decision traces
[p] To a rule and its corrections, or to a close run and its evidence chain. No orphan decisions.
